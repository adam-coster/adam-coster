# Crashlands 2 has a release date! But what's a release date announcement even for?

It's been nearly 5 years since our last game launch, but we finally have the release date for our next one: [Crashlands 2](https://bscotch.net/games/crashlands-2) comes out on April 10, 2025!

But this post isn't about that, it's about the more general question:

> What's the purpose of a release date announcement?

Let's dig into it, using details from the Crashlands 2 announcement as examples.

## Press Beats

What any marketing person will tell you is that attention is all about "press beats". Bits of news that you can fling out into the world at various points leading up to your game launch. Each beat has a different purpose, target audience, and set of goals.

If you misunderstand the appropriate audience for a given press beat, you'll focus on the wrong things and/or evaluate the results incorrectly.

So what are the audiences for a release date announcement, and what are we trying to get them to do?

## Primary Audience: Existing Fans

Anyone who makes games for a living knows that as soon as your upcoming title has fans, you'll be bombarded with the same question every moment of every day: "WHEN DOES IT COME OUT!?!?"

This is the audience who will be, by far, the most impacted by a release date announcement. They just want to play the damn game already, so finally knowing _when_ is the only thing they are about (besides the launch itself).

So you want to reach as many existing fans as possible with this type of announcement, provide something new about the game that they haven't seen before, give them a place to talk about it, and encourage them to sign up for things that will ensure they get notified when the launch happens.

For Crashlands 2 this meant figuring out where our fans are and coming to them. In order of expected impact:

- **Newsletter:** We have a studio newsletter with >80k engaged subscribers, accumulated over many years. I sent the announcement, along with a link to the trailer, calls to go wishlist/pre-order the game on all of the stores we're launching on, and a link to a beta test application. Our newsletter is, by far, the most powerful broadcast mechanism we have for any announcements to our existing players and fans.
  - **Website:** I cross-posted the announcement [to the studio blog](https://www.bscotch.net/post/crashlands-2-release-date) for posterity, ensuring that it's also visible on the homepage for any site visitors to see. Much lower power, but cross-posting is cheap so why not?
- **Discord:** We have [a Discord community](https://discord.com/invite/bscotch) we've built up since the original Crashlands, which is by far our the most engaged subset of our players. So we of course announced here and spent a few days being responsive to player questions.
- **Steam Announcements:** Crashlands 2 and the original both have decent visibility on Steam, so I drafted a version of the newsletter to post on those store pages. For both games, the announcement is prominently shown on the main game page above the description.
  - **Crashlands 2:** 3 days after the announcement, [this post](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/391730/view/514072769031307584?l=english) had 67 likes and 7 comments. Not a lot, but I don't really have a reference for what "good" looks like.
  - **Crashlands:** At the same time, [the same post on the Crashlands page](https://store.steampowered.com/news/app/1401730/view/523079968283951426?l=english) had 588 likes and 41 comments. A strong reminder of the power of cross-game promotion for a sequel!
- **YouTube:** I [posted our new trailer to YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ib7fzLf59vo) (of course, what maniac _wouldn't_?), where our smallish subscriber base has always been responsive to our videos. Of course, usually the only comment they leave on those _other_ videos is, you guessed it, "When does Crashlands 2 come out?" I read every comment for the first few days and responded to many.
- **reddit:** We've had a [Crashlands subreddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/crashlands/) for ages, and announcement posts tend to do alright there despite the low-ish traffic. On announcement day [I posted a link](https://www.reddit.com/r/crashlands/comments/1i25tlg/crashlands_2_has_a_release_date_april_10_and_a/) to the announcement blog post, but it was [the direct upload of the new trailer](https://www.reddit.com/r/crashlands/comments/1i2zpuv/crashlands_2_release_date_announcement_trailer/) the following day that got attention.
- **In-Game Announcement:** We have an in-game news ticker thingy on the main menu of all of our games, so I plopped an announcement in there linking out to the Crashlands 2 website.
- **Social Media:** We're a bit of an outlier as a studio in that we rarely post on social media. But, hey, undoubtedly a subset of our fans are on each social media site, so it's worth breaking out the 2-factor and posting everywhere. The initial announcement posts got more attention than anything we'd posted in a long time, but follow-up posts were basically invisible. So we spent a few days replying to replies and then went back to the comfort of ignoring social media. Targets included BlueSky, Twitter, Facebook, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts.

## Primary Audience: Press & Potential Business Partners

Games press and influencers only cover stuff if they think they can generate revenue for themselves in doing so (it's their job, after all), and so they primarily talk about games and news items that are _already popular_. That's where the audience is, so that's where the ad revenue can be generated.

But they're all competing against each other on those topics, so anyone who can be _early_ to a piece of news that _will_ be popular gets an edge. When people go looking for info on that thing, those sites and influencers who got in early will show up first.
